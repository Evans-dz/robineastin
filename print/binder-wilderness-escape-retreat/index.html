Good to know

House rules and a few things about life in the forest.

Questions and answers
Check-in
4:00 pm, self check-in with a keypad
Checkout
10:00 am
Smoking
Not inside the cabins, including vaping. Outside is fine
Pets
Not allowed
Garbage
All garbage goes in the cans outside next to the cabin. Pickup is Monday and Thursday.
Bears and moose
Grizzlies live around Island Park. On the National Forest, food and trash must be stored in a vehicle, a building or a bear-proof container from March 1 to December 1. Carry bear spray, bring in bird feeders, and give moose plenty of room, especially cows with calves.
Fire
Fireworks are banned on the National Forest. Check current fire restrictions before any campfire.
Altitude
The cabins sit at about 6,300 feet. Headache, nausea or dizziness can be altitude sickness: rest, drink water, and get help if it gets worse.
Weather
July averages 77°F by day and 43°F at night; frost is possible in any month. January averages 27°F by day and 6°F at night, with over 200 inches of snow a year. Pack layers and rain gear in every season.

YellowstoneWilderness Escape Retreat

Yellowstone

The West Entrance at West Yellowstone is about 30 minutes from the cabins and the closest way into the park. No reservation is needed in 2026, just a pass.

Latest road and park news
Entrance fee
$35 per vehicle for 7 days. Yellowstone annual pass $70; America the Beautiful pass $80 for US residents.
From outside the US
New in 2026: $100 per person aged 16 and over on top of the entrance fee, waived with an annual pass. The nonresident annual pass is $250.
Reservations
None needed to get in. There is no timed entry or vehicle reservation in 2026; just buy a pass.
West Entrance
Open to cars April 17 to October 31, 2026. From December 15 to March 15 it is open only to snowcoaches and guided snowmobiles.
Gas and food
There is no gas at Madison or Norris, so fill up in West Yellowstone. Most park food service closes by late October; pack a lunch.
Cell service
Very limited. Download the NPS app and its offline content before you leave the cabins.
Fishing
A Yellowstone permit is required, and state licenses do not count: $40 for 3 days, $55 for 7 days.
  • Stay at least 100 yards from bears and wolves, and 25 yards from every other animal, including bison and elk. If an animal moves because of you, you are too close.
  • Bison have injured more people in Yellowstone than any other animal, and they run three times faster than you.
  • Carry bear spray where you can reach it fast, hike in groups of three or more, and make noise. Never leave the can in a hot car.
  • Stay on boardwalks and marked trails in thermal areas and keep children close. The water can kill, and soaking in hot springs is illegal.

Yellowstone day plans

Five days in the park from our door. Times are one way with no stops; summer days run slower, so add at least a third.

Day plans

Geyser day

About 1 hr 35 min each way to Old Faithful

The classic first day. In through the West Entrance, then south along the Firehole River to Old Faithful, and home the same way.

  1. Firehole Canyon Drive and Firehole Falls
  2. Fountain Paint Pot
  3. Grand Prismatic Spring, plus the overlook or Fairy Falls if your legs are fresh
  4. Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin boardwalks

Be at the entrance before 7 am, or come in after noon, and skip the big stops between 10 and 4. Biscuit Basin and Firehole Lake Drive are closed in 2026.

Canyon and Hayden Valley

About 1 hr 50 min each way to Canyon

Waterfalls and wildlife. For a very long day, keep going around the Lower Loop past the lake and home through Old Faithful.

  1. Norris Geyser Basin (walk Porcelain Basin)
  2. Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone: Artist Point, Lookout Point and the Brink of the Lower Falls
  3. Hayden Valley late in the day for bison, and maybe bears

Canyon’s overlooks change with the light. Try more than one.

Lamar Valley wildlife day

About 2 hr 45 min each way

The wolf watcher’s day. Wolves are most active at dawn and dusk, which means leaving the cabins well before sunrise.

  1. Norris, Canyon, then over Dunraven Pass to Tower-Roosevelt (after October 12, go through Mammoth instead)
  2. Lamar Valley pullouts with binoculars or a spotting scope
  3. Mammoth Hot Springs on the way home

Bridge work east of Tower Junction can add 30 minutes through November 2026.

Mammoth day

About 2 hr 10 min each way

The north side of the park: travertine terraces, history, and in September and October, bugling elk on the lawns.

  1. Norris Geyser Basin
  2. Roaring Mountain and Obsidian Cliff on the drive north
  3. Mammoth terraces, Upper Terrace Drive and Fort Yellowstone

Easy half day

About 1 hr 10 min to the first stop

For arrival day or tired kids: the closest park sights, back at the cabins by afternoon.

  1. The Madison River, watching for elk and bison
  2. Firehole Canyon Drive and Firehole Falls
  3. Fountain Paint Pot

Or skip the park: Big Springs and the Mesa Falls loop are closer to home.

Wildlife: where to look

You will almost certainly see bison and elk. With patience and an early start, maybe wolves and bears. Here is where to look, and how close is too close.

Wildlife guide
  • BisonHayden and Lamar valleys all year; along the Madison River and thermal areas in winter; some live around Old FaithfulAll year. Rut late July to August
  • ElkSummer: Madison Canyon and Lamar. Fall: Mammoth and the Madison RiverRut early September to mid-October, with bugling bulls
  • Grizzly bearHayden and Lamar valleys, the north slopes of Mount Washburn, and from Fishing Bridge to the East EntranceMostly dawn, dusk and night; very active in fall
  • Black bearAround Tower and Mammoth, and toward the Northeast EntranceDawn and dusk, sometimes midday
  • Gray wolfThe northern range, especially Lamar Valley; also Hayden ValleyDawn and dusk
  • MooseThe park’s southwest corner and river bottoms. Near the cabins: Harriman State Park and the Big Springs floatRut late September to early October
  • PronghornLamar Valley and near GardinerBreeding mid-September to early October
  • Bighorn sheepMount Washburn along Dunraven Pass in summer; the canyon near Gardiner all yearRut begins in November
  • Bald eagleBig rivers and lakes; about half of known nests are around Yellowstone LakeSome stay all year
  • Trumpeter swanSlow rivers and quiet lakes. The best spot near the cabins is Harriman State ParkBiggest numbers at Harriman in winter
  • Never feed wildlife, including birds and squirrels.
  • Drones are banned in the park.
  • Pull fully off the road to watch animals. Never stop in a travel lane.
  • Pets stay in developed areas, never on boardwalks or trails.

Emergencies and practical notes

Practical notes
911

Call 911. Island Park has no hospital or 24-hour emergency room.

Tell the dispatcher you are at 3808 Mortensen Ln, Island Park, ID.

Nearest ER
Madison Memorial Hospital, 450 E Main St, Rexburg: about 1 hr 15 min. (208) 359-6900.
Clinic
Island Park Medical Clinic, by Pond’s Lodge on US-20, about 10 min: weekdays 8 to 5, walk-ins welcome, X-ray and stitches. (208) 558-7766.
Pharmacy
There is no pharmacy in Island Park. The nearest are in West Yellowstone and Ashton, both weekdays only.
Useful numbers
Island Park Ranger Station (208) 558-7301. Island Park Chamber of Commerce 208-558-7755. Fremont County Sheriff (208) 624-4482.
Roads
Idaho road conditions: 511.idaho.gov or dial 511. Yellowstone road recording: (307) 344-2117.
Fishing licenses
Idaho: everyone 14 and older needs a license; a nonresident day license is $22.75 for the first day and $7 for each extra day. Yellowstone needs its own permit ($40 for 3 days), and state licenses do not count there. The Henry’s Fork from the Henrys Lake Outlet up to Big Springs is closed to fishing.
Boats and paddleboards
Out-of-state boats, kayaks, canoes, rafts and paddleboards need an Idaho invasive species sticker ($7 non-motorized, $30 motorized), and must stop at any open inspection station.

Checking out

Thanks for staying with us. A few quick things before you head out.

On your phone
  1. Check out by 10:00 am
  2. Leave all the beds unmade
  3. Start a load of towels

    Gather the towels in the laundry room and start a wash. If you have time, move them to the dryer. It helps our cleaner get ready for the next guests.

  4. Wash the dishes and clear the food

    Wash the dishes; the cleaner will put them away. Take your food out of the fridge, freezer and cupboards.

  5. Take out the garbage

    All garbage goes in the cans outside next to the cabin. Pickup is Monday and Thursday.

  6. Lock up

    Shut and lock the windows and doors. Press the lock button on the keypad as you leave.

  7. Sign the guest book

    We love reading about your stay.

  8. Leave us a 5-star review

    Please take time to leave a 5-star review wherever you booked. It helps other families find our cabins.
