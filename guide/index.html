Chapter 1

Yellowstone, from the West Entrance

The West Entrance at West Yellowstone is about 45 minutes from the cabins and the closest way into the park. No reservation is needed in 2026, just a pass.

Before you go in

Entrance fee
$35 per vehicle for 7 days. Yellowstone annual pass $70; America the Beautiful pass $80 for US residents. Source
From outside the US
New in 2026: $100 per person aged 16 and over on top of the entrance fee, waived with an annual pass. The nonresident annual pass is $250. Source
Reservations
None needed to get in. There is no timed entry or vehicle reservation in 2026; just buy a pass. Source
West Entrance
Open to cars April 17 to October 31, 2026. From December 15 to March 15 it is open only to snowcoaches and guided snowmobiles. Source
Road work
Delays in 2026 at the Firehole River Bridge south of Old Faithful (October), near Mammoth, and up to 30 minutes east of Tower Junction through November. Road status: go.nps.gov/yellroads or 307-344-2117. Source
Closed in 2026
Biscuit Basin, Firehole Lake Drive (to cars) and the Firehole swim area, as of September 2026. Source
Gas and food
There is no gas at Madison or Norris, so fill up in West Yellowstone. Most park food service closes by late October; pack a lunch. Source
Cell service
Very limited. Download the NPS app and its offline content before you leave the cabins. Source
Fishing
A Yellowstone permit is required, and state licenses do not count: $40 for 3 days, $55 for 7 days. Source

Day plans from the cabins

  • Geyser day

    About 1 hr 35 min each way to Old Faithful

    The classic first day. In through the West Entrance, then south along the Firehole River to Old Faithful, and home the same way.

    1. Firehole Canyon Drive and Firehole Falls
    2. Fountain Paint Pot
    3. Grand Prismatic Spring, plus the overlook or Fairy Falls if your legs are fresh
    4. Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin boardwalks

    Be at the entrance before 7 am, or come in after noon, and skip the big stops between 10 and 4. Biscuit Basin and Firehole Lake Drive are closed in 2026.

  • Canyon and Hayden Valley

    About 1 hr 50 min each way to Canyon

    Waterfalls and wildlife. For a very long day, keep going around the Lower Loop past the lake and home through Old Faithful.

    1. Norris Geyser Basin (walk Porcelain Basin)
    2. Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone: Artist Point, Lookout Point and the Brink of the Lower Falls
    3. Hayden Valley late in the day for bison, and maybe bears

    Canyon’s overlooks change with the light. Try more than one.

  • Lamar Valley wildlife day

    About 2 hr 45 min each way

    The wolf watcher’s day. Wolves are most active at dawn and dusk, which means leaving the cabins well before sunrise.

    1. Norris, Canyon, then over Dunraven Pass to Tower-Roosevelt (after October 12, go through Mammoth instead)
    2. Lamar Valley pullouts with binoculars or a spotting scope
    3. Mammoth Hot Springs on the way home

    Bridge work east of Tower Junction can add 30 minutes through November 2026.

  • Mammoth day

    About 2 hr 10 min each way

    The north side of the park: travertine terraces, history, and in September and October, bugling elk on the lawns.

    1. Norris Geyser Basin
    2. Roaring Mountain and Obsidian Cliff on the drive north
    3. Mammoth terraces, Upper Terrace Drive and Fort Yellowstone

  • Easy half day

    About 1 hr 10 min to the first stop

    For arrival day or tired kids: the closest park sights, back at the cabins by afternoon.

    1. The Madison River, watching for elk and bison
    2. Firehole Canyon Drive and Firehole Falls
    3. Fountain Paint Pot

    Or skip the park: Big Springs and the Mesa Falls loop are closer to home.

The big sights

Drive times assume no stops and no traffic. Summer days in the park run slower, so add at least a third.

  • Geysers

    Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin

    1 hr 40 min from the cabins

    The park’s most famous geyser shoots about 130 feet, roughly every 100 minutes. Around it, boardwalks wind past at least 150 more geysers in about a square mile, out to Morning Glory Pool.

    Check eruption predictions at the visitor center or in the NPS app. Go before 10 am or after 4 pm.

    Half dayGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Hot spring · Midway Geyser Basin

    Grand Prismatic Spring

    1 hr 25 min from the cabins

    Yellowstone’s largest hot spring, 200 to 330 feet across and ringed in orange. For the famous view from above, climb the short, steep trail to the Grand Prismatic Overlook (1.2 miles round trip).

    Parking is tight from 10 am to 6 pm. Go early or late.

    1 to 2 hrGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Waterfall hike

    Fairy Falls

    1 hr 25 min from the cabins

    A 200-foot waterfall at the end of a 5.4-mile round trip from the Fairy Falls trailhead, which also passes the Grand Prismatic Overlook.

    The trailhead lot is small and fills early. Bear country: carry bear spray.

    3 hr

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Boardwalk

    Fountain Paint Pot

    1 hr 20 min from the cabins

    A short boardwalk with all four kinds of thermal feature in one stop: geysers, hot springs, steam vents and bubbling mudpots.

    30 to 45 minGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Scenic drive

    Firehole Canyon Drive

    1 hr 10 min from the cabins

    A one-way side road through a canyon cut into lava flows 800 feet thick, past 40-foot Firehole Falls.

    The swim area was closed in 2026. Check its status before you pack swimsuits.

    20 to 40 minGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Wildlife and fishing

    Madison River

    1 hr 10 min from the cabins

    The road in from West Yellowstone follows the Madison, a classic fly-fishing river. Watch for bison along the banks and, in fall, bugling bull elk.

    Fishing in the park needs a Yellowstone permit, not a state license.

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Geysers

    Norris Geyser Basin

    1 hr 30 min from the cabins

    The hottest and most changeable thermal area in the park, and home of Steamboat, the world’s tallest active geyser.

    1 to 2 hrGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Canyon and waterfalls

    Grand Canyon of the Yellowstone

    1 hr 55 min from the cabins

    A yellow-walled canyon with the 308-foot Lower Falls. See it from Artist Point on the South Rim, or take the switchbacks 600 feet down to the Brink of the Lower Falls.

    Sunny days put rainbows in the spray. The climb back up from the brink is steep.

    Half dayGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Wildlife valley

    Hayden Valley

    2 hr from the cabins

    Open valley along the Yellowstone River between Canyon and the lake: bison herds, elk and birds, and sometimes bears and wolves.

    Best at dawn and dusk. This road is open to cars May 1 to October 31.

    Good with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Wildlife valley

    Lamar Valley

    2 hr 45 min from the cabins

    The wide valley on the northeast road is known for huge bison and elk herds and some of the best wolf watching in the world.

    A long day from Island Park: leave before dawn and bring binoculars or a spotting scope.

    Good with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Hot springs

    Mammoth Hot Springs Terraces

    2 hr 10 min from the cabins

    Stepped travertine terraces that change from year to year, on boardwalks and the one-way Upper Terrace Drive, beside historic Fort Yellowstone.

    Elk often graze the lawns here, especially during the fall rut.

    1 to 2 hrGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Lakeshore geysers

    West Thumb Geyser Basin

    2 hr 5 min from the cabins

    Hot springs right on the shore of Yellowstone Lake, some of them underwater, on an easy 0.6-mile boardwalk loop.

    45 minGood with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Summit hike

    Mount Washburn

    2 hr 5 min from the cabins

    A fire lookout on the summit with some of the biggest views in the park, about 6 miles round trip from Dunraven Pass.

    The Dunraven Pass road is open late May to mid-October.

    4 to 5 hr

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Waterfall

    Tower Fall

    2 hr 30 min from the cabins

    A 132-foot waterfall a short walk from the Tower general store.

    The trail to the base is closed.

    30 minGood with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Snowcoach and snowmobile tours · From West Yellowstone

    Yellowstone in winter

    40 min from the cabins

    From mid-December to mid-March the West Entrance is open only to snowcoaches and guided snowmobiles. Tours leave from West Yellowstone for Old Faithful and the geyser basins in the snow.

    Winter 2026-27 over-snow season: Dec 15 to Mar 15.

    Best: WinterOfficial info

  • Big day trip · Wyoming

    Grand Teton and Jackson

    2 hr 40 min from the cabins

    The Tetons rise to 13,775 feet just south of Yellowstone. The quickest way from the cabins is through Idaho and over Teton Pass. The best day is a loop: through Yellowstone one way and over the pass the other, or stay a night.

    Grand Teton charges its own $35 fee. Check wyoroad.info before crossing Teton Pass.

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

Park rules that matter

  • Stay at least 100 yards from bears and wolves, and 25 yards from every other animal, including bison and elk. If an animal moves because of you, you are too close.
  • Bison have injured more people in Yellowstone than any other animal, and they run three times faster than you.
  • Carry bear spray where you can reach it fast, hike in groups of three or more, and make noise. Never leave the can in a hot car.
  • Stay on boardwalks and marked trails in thermal areas and keep children close. The water can kill, and soaking in hot springs is illegal.
  • Never feed wildlife, including birds and squirrels.
  • Drones are banned in the park.
  • Pull fully off the road to watch animals. Never stop in a travel lane.
  • Pets stay in developed areas, never on boardwalks or trails.
Chapter 2

Island Park, outside the park

You do not have to enter Yellowstone to have a full week. Springs, waterfalls, famous fly fishing and hundreds of miles of trails start close to the cabins.

  • Lake and boating · Next to the cabins

    Island Park Reservoir

    5 min from the cabins

    The big reservoir on the Henry’s Fork is minutes from the cabins, with boat ramps at McCrea Bridge and Buttermilk. Boat, paddle and fish for trout and kokanee in summer, or ice fish near the dam in winter.

    The water drops through late summer. By August the McCrea Bridge ramp no longer reaches it, so use Buttermilk.

    Any lengthFreeGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, WinterOfficial info

  • Springs and history · Mack’s Inn area

    Big Springs and Johnny Sack Cabin

    25 min from the cabins

    One of the largest springs in the country pours out a steady 52°F river: the start of the Henry’s Fork. Watch huge protected trout from the footbridge, walk the flat riverside trail, and look inside a hand-built 1930s log cabin.

    Johnny Sack Cabin is open mid-June to Labor Day. No fishing at the spring.

    30 to 90 minFreeGood with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Float trip · Big Springs to Mack’s Inn

    Big Springs Water Trail float

    15 min from the cabins

    A calm, clear float of about 5 miles down the spring-fed upper Henry’s Fork, ending just above Mack’s Inn. Moose, eagles and sandhill cranes are common along the way.

    Mack’s Inn River Adventures rents boats with the shuttle, Memorial Day to Labor Day. Life jackets on everyone.

    2 to 4 hrRentals from $95 (2026)Good with kids

    Best: SummerOfficial info

  • State park and wildlife

    Harriman State Park

    30 min from the cabins

    A historic ranch turned 11,000-acre park inside a wildlife refuge, with moose, elk, eagles and trumpeter swans along 8 miles of the Henry’s Fork. Summer brings ranch tours on weekends and 22 miles of trails.

    Dogs are allowed only in the parking lots.

    2 hr to a full day$7 per vehicleGood with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, SpringOfficial info

  • Skiing and swans · Harriman State Park

    Harriman in winter: swans and ski trails

    30 min from the cabins

    When the park’s lakes freeze, trumpeter swans gather on the open river, sometimes by the hundreds. Ski or snowshoe more than 20 miles of groomed trail, or rent a rustic yurt.

    Half day or more$6 per person per day, plus $7 per vehicleGood with kids

    Best: WinterOfficial info

  • Waterfalls · Mesa Falls Scenic Byway

    Upper and Lower Mesa Falls

    50 min from the cabins

    The Henry’s Fork drops about 110 feet at Upper Mesa Falls, one of the few big Western waterfalls never dammed. Boardwalks run to the brink, and Lower Mesa Falls is a mile downstream from the Grandview overlook.

    Make it a loop: US-20 south, the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway, Ashton, then back north. Closed to cars in winter.

    45 to 90 min$5 per vehicle at Upper FallsGood with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Fly fishing · Below Island Park Dam

    Henry’s Fork: Box Canyon

    20 min from the cabins

    Three miles of fast water in a basalt canyon below Island Park Dam, holding big wild rainbows and a famous early-June salmonfly hatch. Wading is rough, so float it with a guide if you are new to it.

    Idaho fishing license

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Fly fishing · Harriman State Park

    Henry’s Fork: the Ranch

    30 min from the cabins

    Inside Harriman State Park the river slows into glassy, wadeable water with large, picky rainbows: one of the most famous dry-fly stretches in the country.

    Local fly shops give June 15 as the opener. Confirm it in the current Idaho fishing rules.

    Idaho fishing license plus park fee

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Hike · Upper Coffeepot campground

    Coffee Pot Rapids Trail

    20 min from the cabins

    An easy riverside trail of about 2.5 miles, out to where the calm Henry’s Fork turns into whitewater. Wildflowers early in summer, huckleberries later.

    Keep kids well back from the rapids.

    1.5 to 3 hrFreeGood with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Scenic drive · North of Mack’s Inn

    Sawtell Peak drive

    1 hr from the cabins

    A 13-mile gravel road climbs to nearly 9,900 feet. On a clear day you can see Island Park’s ancient caldera, the reservoir and Henrys Lake, and into Yellowstone and the Tetons. Wildflowers peak in July and August.

    The road is open to the public June 1 to October 31. Bring a warm layer for the top.

    2 to 3 hr round tripFreeGood with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Lake and fishing

    Henrys Lake State Park

    30 min from the cabins

    A big mountain lake ringed on three sides by the Continental Divide, famous for trophy cutthroat, brook and hybrid trout. The park has a boat ramp, loaner fishing rods and easy nature trails.

    Trout are catch-and-release from Feb 15 until Memorial Day weekend. Check the current rules.

    1 hr to a full day$7 per vehicleGood with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Tubing and biking · Near Ashton

    Warm River

    1 hr from the cabins

    A small, spring-fed river made for lazy tubing, with an old railroad grade alongside for easy biking and walking.

    Good with kids

    Best: SummerOfficial info

  • Waterfall · Yellowstone’s southwest corner, via Ashton

    Cave Falls

    1 hr 40 min from the cabins

    Only about 20 feet tall but some 250 feet wide, it is often called Yellowstone’s widest waterfall. It sits in the park’s quiet Bechler corner, and the last 12 miles of road are gravel.

    It is inside the national park, so park and bear rules apply.

    Half day with the driveGood with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Visitor center · Montana, below Hebgen Lake

    Earthquake Lake Visitor Center

    55 min from the cabins

    Just before midnight on August 17, 1959, a magnitude 7.3 earthquake brought down a mountainside, dammed the Madison River and made a new lake. The visitor center tells the story beside the slide.

    Open Memorial Day to September 15, 10 am to 5 pm.

    1 hrFreeGood with kids

    Best: SummerOfficial info

  • Winter

    Snowmobiling

    Island Park is one of the best-known snowmobile areas in the West, with more than 500 miles of groomed trail that connect to hundreds more around West Yellowstone. Local shops rent sleds and run guided tours.

    Trails are groomed roughly December to March. Out-of-state sleds need an Idaho nonresident certificate ($59.50).

    Best: WinterOfficial info

  • Snowmobile ride · Idaho and Montana line

    Two Top

    A high ride on the Idaho and Montana line known for “snow ghosts”, trees frosted solid with rime, and big views over Henrys Lake. Best for experienced riders or with a guide.

    Best: WinterOfficial info

  • Off-road

    ATV and UTV trails

    The local ranger district has about 615 miles of roads open to motorized travel, from forest two-tracks to the Henrys Lake Flat trails. Several Island Park shops rent machines.

    Riders under 16 need a safety course and a licensed adult close by. Ask the rental shop about permits.

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Trail rides

    Horseback riding

    Guided one- and two-hour rides and wagon rides at Harriman State Park with Haviland’s Old West Adventures, or rides and cowboy cookouts with Yellowstone Horses.

    Riders usually need to be 8 or older; wagon rides suit younger kids.

    Good with kids

    Best: SummerOfficial info

  • Foraging

    Huckleberry picking

    Wild huckleberries ripen in the forest around Island Park in late summer; the Coffee Pot Rapids trail is one known spot. Pick for your own use.

    Bears love them too. Carry bear spray and make noise.

    Good with kids

    Best: SummerOfficial info

Wildlife: where, when, how close

You will almost certainly see bison and elk. With patience and an early start, maybe wolves and bears. Here is where to look, and how close is too close.

Chapter 4

Shows, museums and rainy days

For evenings, rainy afternoons and kids who need a break from the car. (The IMAX theatre in West Yellowstone closed for good in January 2026.)

  • Family musicals · Island Park, Mack’s Inn area

    The Playmill Theatre

    The family musical theatre that played West Yellowstone since 1964 moved into a new, much bigger theatre in Island Park for 2026, with a steakhouse inside. Summer musicals, a fall play and a Christmas show.

    Check the schedule before you come; the new building plans to run year-round.

    About $40 a seat (fall 2026)Good with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, WinterOfficial info

  • Dinner theatre · Island Park, above Henrys Lake

    Yellowstone Playhouse

    Family-friendly musical spoofs and variety shows with an optional dinner, nightly except Sundays from Memorial Day to Labor Day.

    Good with kids

    Best: SummerOfficial info

  • Wildlife center · West Yellowstone

    Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center

    40 min from the cabins

    A nonprofit, accredited wildlife center with grizzlies, wolves, otters and birds of prey that could not survive in the wild. The bears do not hibernate here, so it is just as good in winter.

    Open every day of the year. Your ticket is good for two days in a row.

    1.5 to 2 hrAdults $17.50, ages 5 to 12 $12.50Good with kids

    Best: Summer, Fall, Winter, SpringOfficial info

  • History museum · West Yellowstone

    Museum of the Yellowstone

    40 min from the cabins

    Park history in the old Union Pacific depot: early travel, bears, the 1959 earthquake and the 1988 fires.

    Open mid-May to mid-October.

    1 to 2 hrAdults $10, under 18 free (2026)Good with kids

    Best: Summer, FallOfficial info

  • Rodeo · US-20, west of West Yellowstone

    Wild West Yellowstone Rodeo

    Summer-evening rodeo with bronc and bull riding, roping and a kids' calf scramble.

    Mid-June through August. Gates open at 6 pm, rodeo at 7.

    2 to 3 hrAdults $25, ages 3 to 11 $15 (2026)Good with kids

    Best: SummerOfficial info

Chapter 8

Good to know

Emergencies
Call 911. Island Park has no hospital or 24-hour emergency room.
Nearest ER
Madison Memorial Hospital, 450 E Main St, Rexburg: about 1 hr 15 min. (208) 359-6900. Source
Clinic
Island Park Medical Clinic, by Pond’s Lodge on US-20, about 10 min: weekdays 8 to 5, walk-ins welcome, X-ray and stitches. (208) 558-7766. Source
Pharmacy
There is none in Island Park. The nearest are in West Yellowstone and Ashton, both weekdays only. Source
Cell service
Patchy in the forest. Download offline maps and your booking details before you arrive. Source
Altitude
The cabins sit at about 6,300 feet. Headache, nausea or dizziness can be altitude sickness: rest, drink water, and get help if it gets worse. Source
Weather
July averages 77°F by day and 43°F at night; frost is possible in any month. January averages 27°F by day and 6°F at night, with over 200 inches of snow a year. Pack layers and rain gear in every season. Source
Fishing licenses
Idaho: everyone 14 and older needs a license; a nonresident day license is $22.75 for the first day and $7 for each extra day. Yellowstone needs its own permit ($40 for 3 days), and state licenses do not count there. The Henry’s Fork from the Henrys Lake Outlet up to Big Springs is closed to fishing. Source
Boats and paddleboards
Out-of-state boats, kayaks, canoes, rafts and paddleboards need an Idaho invasive species sticker ($7 non-motorized, $30 motorized), and must stop at any open inspection station. Source
Roads
Idaho road conditions: 511.idaho.gov or dial 511. Yellowstone road recording: (307) 344-2117. Source
Bears and moose
Grizzlies live around Island Park. On the National Forest, food and trash must be stored in a vehicle, a building or a bear-proof container from March 1 to December 1. Carry bear spray, bring in bird feeders, and give moose plenty of room, especially cows with calves. Source
Fire
Fireworks are banned on the National Forest. Check current fire restrictions before any campfire. Source
Useful numbers
Island Park Ranger Station (208) 558-7301. Island Park Chamber of Commerce 208-558-7755. Fremont County Sheriff (208) 624-4482. Source
